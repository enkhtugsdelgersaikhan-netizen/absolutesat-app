Form, Structure, and Sense

Standard English
What it tests

This skill covers grammar and usage beyond punctuation: subject-verb agreement, pronouns, verb tense and form, modifiers, possessives, parallelism, comparisons, and sentence structure. The correct choice must fit both grammatical rules and the meaning/time relationships of the sentence.

How the SAT disguises it
  • A long phrase separates the subject from its verb.
  • A plural noun near the verb distracts from a singular subject.
  • Verb tense must match a time clue or another verb.
  • A modifier is placed next to the wrong noun.
  • Items in a list are not grammatically parallel.
Example problem

The collection of meteorite fragments, along with several detailed field notes, ______ stored in a climate-controlled archive.

  • A) are
  • B) were
  • C) is
  • D) have been
Answer: C
Deep-dive solution
  1. Find the grammatical subject: 'collection,' not 'fragments' or 'notes.'
  2. 'Collection' is singular.
  3. The phrase 'of meteorite fragments' and the interrupting phrase 'along with several detailed field notes' do not change the subject's number.
  4. Therefore the singular verb 'is' agrees with the subject.
Fast method

Cross out prepositional phrases and interrupting phrases when checking subject-verb agreement. Then match the true subject to the verb.

Common trap

The noun closest to the verb is often a deliberate distractor. Agreement follows the grammatical subject, not the nearest noun.
