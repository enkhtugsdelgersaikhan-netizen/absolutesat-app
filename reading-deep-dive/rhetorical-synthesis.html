Rhetorical Synthesis

Expression of Ideas
What it tests

Rhetorical Synthesis questions provide a student's notes and a specific writing goal. Your job is to choose the sentence that uses only relevant information and arranges it to accomplish that goal. The same notes can produce different correct sentences depending on whether the goal is to emphasize a contrast, introduce an example, explain a chronology, define a term, or support a claim.

How the SAT disguises it
  • All choices may be factually true, but only one satisfies the stated goal.
  • A choice includes extra facts that distract from the requested focus.
  • The goal asks for a comparison, but a choice mentions only one item.
  • The notes contain dates, names, and details that must be selectively combined.
Example problem

Notes: • Architect Lina Bo Bardi designed the São Paulo Museum of Art. • The museum's main gallery is suspended above an open public plaza. • The design preserves ground-level space for gatherings and markets. Goal: Emphasize how the building's structure supports public use. Which choice best accomplishes the goal?

  • A) Lina Bo Bardi was an architect who designed a museum in São Paulo.
  • B) The São Paulo Museum of Art includes a main gallery and a public plaza.
  • C) By suspending the main gallery above the ground, Bo Bardi's design preserves an open plaza for public gatherings and markets.
  • D) Markets and public gatherings can occur in many kinds of urban spaces.
Answer: C
Deep-dive solution
  1. Identify the requested relationship: structure → public use.
  2. The key structural fact is the suspended gallery. The key public-use fact is the preserved plaza for gatherings and markets.
  3. Choice C explicitly connects those facts with cause-and-effect wording.
  4. A and B are true but do not fulfill the goal; D is generic and not grounded in the notes.
Fast method

Ignore the notes at first and underline the goal. Then select only the note fragments necessary to achieve that goal.

Common trap

The correct answer is not the sentence with the most facts. It is the sentence with the most goal-relevant facts.
