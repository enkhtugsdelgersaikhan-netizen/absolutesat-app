What SAT scores mean

Composite
1560–1600Elite. SAT is effectively never going to be the problem in admissions.
1500–1550Near elite. Enough for essentially every school; more mainly helps offset weaker academics or unusually score-heavy programs.
1450–1490Highly competitive. Strong for most selective schools, though still below typical scores at some of the most selective.
1400–1440Competitive. Strong at many selective schools, but can become a relative weakness at the top end.
1300–1390School-dependent. Helpful at many universities, but often below the submitted-score range at highly selective schools.
1200–1290Limited at highly selective schools. Useful at many colleges, but usually not an admissions strength at the most selective ones.
400–1190Usually not a selective-school strength. Its value depends heavily on the school’s own score range.

About the data: SAT values prefer the latest usable Common Data Set; when a school does not currently report testing, LexLogica uses the latest available IPEDS testing fields or derives missing values from the reported section/composite range. Every displayed SAT score is normalized to a 10-point increment—no values ending in 5. A derived median is the rounded midpoint of the lower and upper quartiles when no true median is published. Test-optional and test-free policies can make historical or submitter-only ranges unrepresentative of the full entering class.
