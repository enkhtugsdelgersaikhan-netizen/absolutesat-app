Equivalent expressions

Advanced Math
What it tests

Equivalent-expression questions test whether you can rewrite algebra without changing its value. This includes factoring, expanding, combining rational expressions, exponent and radical rules, and recognizing algebraic structure. The hardest versions ask which form reveals a specific feature such as a zero, coefficient, growth factor, or constant.

How the SAT disguises it
  • A polynomial must be factored to expose roots.
  • An expression contains rational or negative exponents.
  • A rational expression has a hidden common factor.
  • An identity contains an unknown coefficient that can be found by matching terms.
Useful structures include a² − b² = (a − b)(a + b), (a ± b)² = a² ± 2ab + b², and a^(m/n) = ⁿ√(a^m).
Example problem

Which expression is equivalent to 9x² − 24x + 16?

  • A) (3x − 4)²
  • B) (9x − 4)²
  • C) (3x + 4)²
  • D) (9x − 16)(x − 1)
Answer: A) (3x − 4)²
Deep-dive solution
  1. Recognize the first and last terms as squares: 9x² = (3x)² and 16 = 4².
  2. A perfect-square trinomial with a negative middle term has form a² − 2ab + b².
  3. Here −2(3x)(4) = −24x, exactly matching the middle term.
  4. Therefore 9x² − 24x + 16 = (3x − 4)².
Fast method

Before expanding anything, inspect the first term, last term, and middle coefficient for common identities. Structural recognition is much faster than brute force.

Common trap

You may cancel factors in a rational expression, not terms separated by addition or subtraction. Also remember √(x²) = |x|, not simply x for every real x.
