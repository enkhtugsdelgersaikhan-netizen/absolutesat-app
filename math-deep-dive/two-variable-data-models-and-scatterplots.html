Two-variable data: models and scatterplots

PSDA
What it tests

This skill tests relationships between two variables. You may interpret direction and strength of association, choose a linear/quadratic/exponential model, use a model for prediction, interpret slope or other parameters, or calculate and interpret residuals. The SAT may also ask whether extrapolation is sensible.

How the SAT disguises it
  • A scatterplot suggests a model type.
  • A line of best fit is used to make a prediction.
  • An observed point is compared with a predicted point.
  • The wording tries to turn association into causation.
For a linear model ŷ = mx + b, residual = observed y − predicted ŷ.
Example problem

A model predicts that a plant will be 42 cm tall after 6 weeks. Its actual height after 6 weeks is 47 cm. What is the residual?

  • A) −5
  • B) 5
  • C) 42
  • D) 89
Answer: B) 5
Deep-dive solution
  1. Residual is observed minus predicted, not the other way around.
  2. Observed height = 47; predicted height = 42.
  3. Residual = 47 − 42 = 5.
  4. A positive residual means the observed point lies above the model's prediction.
Fast method

Memorize 'residual = real minus model.' Positive → point above the fitted model; negative → point below.

Common trap

Correlation or a good-fitting model does not by itself establish causation. Also be cautious about predictions far beyond the x-values actually observed.
