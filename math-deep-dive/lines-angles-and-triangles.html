Lines, angles, and triangles

Geometry & Trig
What it tests

This skill tests angle relationships, parallel lines cut by a transversal, triangle angle sums, exterior angles, congruence, similarity, and scale factor. Many questions are less about computation and more about recognizing which theorem makes a missing angle or side determined.

How the SAT disguises it
  • A diagram is not drawn to scale.
  • Parallel-line marks make corresponding or alternate-interior angles equal.
  • Two equal angles establish AA similarity.
  • A side proportion depends on matching corresponding vertices correctly.
Triangle interior angles sum to 180°. Vertical angles are equal. If lines are parallel, corresponding and alternate-interior angles are equal; same-side interior angles sum to 180°.
Example problem

Two parallel lines are cut by a transversal. One acute angle formed is 68°. What is the measure of each obtuse angle formed by the transversal?

  • A) 22°
  • B) 68°
  • C) 112°
  • D) 136°
Answer: C) 112°
Deep-dive solution
  1. With parallel lines, all acute angles formed by the transversal are congruent, so each acute angle is 68°.
  2. Each adjacent acute/obtuse pair forms a straight angle of 180°.
  3. Obtuse angle = 180° − 68° = 112°.
  4. Vertical and corresponding angle relationships then repeat that same obtuse measure at the other positions.
Fast method

In a standard parallel-lines/transversal diagram, once you know one non-right angle θ, every angle is either θ or 180° − θ.

Common trap

Do not trust the visual size of an angle. Only marks, given measures, and theorems justify equality.
