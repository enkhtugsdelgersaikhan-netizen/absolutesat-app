Nonlinear functions

Advanced Math
What it tests

This skill tests quadratic, exponential, polynomial, rational, and other nonlinear functions as relationships rather than isolated equations. You may interpret constants, compare models, identify a maximum or minimum, transform a graph, connect equations to tables, or choose a form that reveals a desired feature.

How the SAT disguises it
  • Vertex form reveals a quadratic maximum/minimum.
  • Factored form reveals zeros.
  • An exponential growth factor must be converted to a percent rate.
  • A graph or table must be identified as linear, quadratic, or exponential.
  • A transformed function shifts, reflects, or stretches a parent graph.
Quadratic vertex form: f(x) = a(x − h)² + k. Exponential form: f(x) = ab^x, where a = f(0) and b is the factor for each +1 change in x.
Example problem

A population is modeled by P(t) = 480(1.06)^t, where t is years after 2026. Which statement is true?

  • A) The population starts at 480 and increases by 6% each year.
  • B) The population starts at 480 and increases by 106% each year.
  • C) The population starts at 1.06 and increases by 480 each year.
  • D) The population starts at 480 and increases by 0.06 people each year.
Answer: A) The population starts at 480 and increases by 6% each year.
Deep-dive solution
  1. At t = 0, P(0) = 480(1.06)^0 = 480, so 480 is the initial value.
  2. The base 1.06 is the multiplicative factor per year.
  3. A factor of 1.06 means retain 100% and add 6%, so the annual growth rate is 6%.
  4. The model is exponential because the same percentage, not the same number, is added each year.
Fast method

For ab^x, ask two questions immediately: what is a, and what does b mean? b = 1 + r for growth and b = 1 − r for decay.

Common trap

Do not confuse a 1.06 multiplier with 106% growth. It represents 106% of the previous value, which is a 6% increase.
