Linear functions

Algebra
What it tests

This skill tests whether you understand a linear function as a constant rate of change plus an initial value. You may need to connect a table, graph, equation, or context; determine slope or intercept; interpret units; or compare two linear models. The SAT often cares more about what the slope and intercept mean than about simply calculating them.

How the SAT disguises it
  • A table has equal x-steps and asks for a missing output.
  • A context gives an initial fee plus a per-unit charge.
  • A graph asks for rate of change or an input that produces a target output.
  • Two linear functions are compared by slope, intercept, or intersection.
For f(x) = mx + b, m is the change in output per 1 unit of input, and b = f(0).
Example problem

A water tank contains 860 liters at 9:00 a.m. and 710 liters at 11:30 a.m. The amount of water decreases at a constant rate. At what rate, in liters per hour, is the amount changing?

  • A) −40
  • B) −50
  • C) −60
  • D) −75
Answer: C) −60
Deep-dive solution
  1. The output is liters and the input is time, so slope means liters per hour.
  2. The amount changes by 710 − 860 = −150 liters.
  3. The elapsed time is 2.5 hours, not 2 hours.
  4. Slope = −150 / 2.5 = −60 liters per hour. The negative sign indicates the tank is losing water.
Fast method

Before calculating slope in a word problem, say the units aloud: 'output units per input unit.' That immediately tells you what should be in the numerator and denominator.

Common trap

Do not ignore unequal time intervals. A graph can also show a y-intercept that exists algebraically but has no sensible contextual meaning if x = 0 lies outside the situation.
